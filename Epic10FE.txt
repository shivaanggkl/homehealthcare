Epic 10 Frontend Backlog
HomeHealthCare

Purpose

This document converts Epic 10 from `Requirements.txt` into a frontend delivery backlog for the QA and review workspace.

Epic 10 title

QA / review workspace

Epic 10 feature scope from the requirements

- review queue
- returned-for-fix workflow
- approve / reject / request-signoff
- completeness checks
- missing-field detection
- exception queue
- audit trail of edits
- role-based review assignment

Planning assumptions

- Epic 1 frontend and backend are complete and provide auth, session, RBAC, protected-route, and audit-view patterns
- Epic 3 frontend and backend are complete and provide patient workspaces and patient context that review screens may reference
- Epic 5 frontend and backend are complete and provide visit and schedule context that QA screens may link back into
- Epic 6 and Epic 7 frontend and backend are complete and provide field execution, EVV, missed-visit, and exception outputs that may appear in reviewable work
- Epic 8 frontend and backend are complete and provide the primary documentation records, draft/submit flows, printable summaries, and attachment linkage that QA will review
- Epic 9 frontend and backend are complete and provide secure coordination patterns that Epic 10 may link to for reviewer follow-up
- Epic 10 frontend should support focused reviewer triage and decision-making, not the broader clinical compliance dashboard from Epic 11 or revenue-readiness workflows from Epic 14
- review screens should stay privacy-aware and show only the minimum patient, visit, and document context needed for triage and decision-making

Status legend

- BLOCKED: frontend should wait for the corresponding Epic 10 backend APIs

========================================================================
FE10-01 — Review workspace information architecture and route model
Related Epic 10 features: all
Backend status: BLOCKED

Story
As a reviewer or operations lead, I want the QA workspace routes to fit naturally into the app so queue triage, review detail, and returned-for-fix work are easy to find.

Frontend acceptance criteria

- protected review workspace route exists
- dedicated routes exist for:
  - review queue
  - exception queue
  - review item detail
  - returned-for-fix or resubmission visibility
  - reviewer assignment views where appropriate
- unsupported or unauthorized review routes render controlled states
- queue and detail routes can link back into related patient, visit, documentation, EVV, and messaging context where authorized

========================================================================
FE10-02 — Review queue triage UX
Related Epic 10 features: review queue
Backend status: BLOCKED

Story
As a QA reviewer, I want a review queue so I can triage pending work, identify priority items, and open the right records quickly.

Frontend acceptance criteria

- review queue screen exists
- queue shows at least:
  - source type
  - patient or visit context where appropriate
  - status
  - assigned reviewer
  - age or entered-queue time
  - priority or urgency where present
- queue supports loading, empty, and failure states clearly
- review queue uses real backend APIs rather than placeholder local state

FE10-10 — Shared review mutation and resubmission UX
Related Epic 10 features: approve / reject / request-signoff, returned-for-fix workflow
Backend status: BLOCKED

Story
As the frontend team, I want one mutation pattern for assignment, decision, return, and signoff actions so review behavior stays predictable and safe.

Frontend acceptance criteria

- review mutations use a consistent pending/success/failure pattern
- retry behavior is explicit for recoverable failures
- high-impact actions such as reject or return-for-fix are confirmed clearly
- refreshed queue/detail state is shown after backend success
- failed review mutations remain understandable and retryable where practical

Definition of done for Epic 10 frontend

Epic 10 frontend is done only when:

- review queue, exception queue, detail, assignment, signoff, and return-for-fix screens are reachable through coherent protected routes
- queue, findings, assignment, decision, and signoff flows use real backend APIs rather than placeholder local state
- completeness and missing-field findings are shown clearly and consistently
- returned, resubmitted, assigned, approved, rejected, and signoff-requested states are visible and understandable
- coordinator-facing review visibility exists for authorized users
- privacy-sensitive patient, visit, and documentation context is minimized appropriately
- review screens are usable on smaller operational devices
- core Epic 10 screens are regression-covered
