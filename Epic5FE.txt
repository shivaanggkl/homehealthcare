Epic 5 Frontend Backlog
HomeHealthCare

Purpose

This document converts Epic 5 from `Requirements.txt` into a frontend delivery backlog for the web application.

Epic 5 title

Scheduling engine

Epic 5 feature scope from the requirements

- day/week/month schedule views
- drag-and-drop assignment
- caregiver-patient matching
- conflict detection
- overtime and overlap checks
- travel-time awareness
- recurring visits
- open-shift pool
- rescheduling workflow
- cancellation workflow

Planning assumptions

- Epic 1 frontend and backend are complete and provide the app shell, permissions, navigation, audit viewer, and protected-route patterns
- Epic 2 setup data is available and reusable where schedule screens need visit types, service lines, branch policies, alert rules, or mileage/travel assumptions
- Epic 3 patient workflows are complete and provide patient records that schedule items can link back to
- Epic 4 workforce workflows are complete and provide caregiver availability, unavailability, geography, skills, credentials, and performance signals for scheduling inputs
- Epic 5 is primarily an admin and coordinator scheduling-board experience in the web application
- Epic 5 should not absorb mobile field execution, GPS proof, signatures, or EVV workflows because those belong to Epics 6 and 7
- scheduling UI should surface explanations for assignment decisions and conflicts rather than opaque pass/fail outcomes
- schedule interactions must degrade gracefully when some advanced backend rule engines are available only as warnings or preview contracts

Status legend

- BLOCKED: frontend should wait for the corresponding Epic 5 backend APIs

Definition of done for Epic 5 frontend

Epic 5 frontend is done only when:

- scheduling records are reachable from the app shell through a dedicated scheduling workspace
- Epic 5 screens use backend APIs rather than placeholder local state
- day, week, and month schedule views are usable and permission-aware
- visit create/edit, assignment, open-shift, recurring, reschedule, and cancellation workflows are usable and permission-aware
- conflict, warning, validation, and unauthorized states are shown clearly
- schedule board navigation, detail surfaces, and mutation patterns are coherent and reusable
- audit-sensitive schedule changes are reflected in the UI
- core Epic 5 screens are regression-covered
